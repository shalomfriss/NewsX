# Indiana GOP’s Trump rebuke could lead to temporary redistricting detente

## Summary

It doesn’t mean the redistricting wars are over.

## Content

<p>Indiana Republicans’ redistricting rejection marks a rare ceasefire in the gerrymandering wars — and could lead to other state leaders backing off their own plans.</p> 
<p>The result gives cover for some Democratic-leaning states to stand down, even as the party’s base is frenzied over the issue. Lawmakers in Illinois and Maryland have for months had internal debates about whether to move forward with redrawing their maps. Indiana’s outcome relieved some of the mounting pressure they anticipated facing had Republicans in Indiana further gerrymandered their maps.</p> 
<p>Illinois Democrats have long said they would only gerrymander if the Indiana GOP bowed to Trump’s demands and redid their own map. In the wake of Hoosier Republicans’ move Thursday, their Democratic neighbors don’t seem eager to change their minds.</p> 
<p>Meanwhile in Maryland, <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/02/bill-ferguson-wes-moore-00664770" target="_blank">one Democratic leader</a> is rebuffing entreaties from top Democrats to eliminate the state’s lone remaining GOP seat.</p> 
<p>Maryland Democratic Senate President Bill Ferguson has exchanged phone calls with Indiana Senate Republican leader Rodric Bray, four people familiar with the two leaders, granted anonymity because they weren't authorized to speak publicly, told POLITICO. Each resisted pressure from top officials in their party to move on redistricting. Bray’s success could now lessen the pressure on Ferguson. Bray's spokesperson, Molly Swigart, <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/11/maryland-redistricting-state-legislature-00687992" target="_blank">said no deal was ever made</a> between Bray and Ferguson on redistricting in their respective states.</p> 
<p>Officials in Virginia, where Democrats gained 13 seats in their House of Delegates in November’s statewide elections, are poised to make drastic changes to their congressional maps that could net the party upwards of four seats. But Democratic Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/09/spanberger-redistricting-00683066" target="_blank">sounded reluctant to the idea</a> of making wholesale changes to congressional lines at a POLITICO event earlier this week.</p> 
<p>There are headwinds elsewhere for Trump and his allies. In <a href="https://www.kcur.org/politics-elections-and-government/2025-11-05/kansas-republicans-fail-to-get-support-for-redistricting-but-their-plan-isnt-dead-yet" target="_blank">Kansas</a> and Kentucky Republicans have so far failed to move forward with their redistricting pushes that are complicated by opposition from Democratic governors. Ohio Republicans struck a compromise with Democrats for a <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/10/31/ohio-redistricting-gerrymander-new-map-00631254" target="_blank">less aggressive gerrymander</a> than what some national leaders wanted. And a judge picked a map in Utah that drew <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/08/29/democrats-seize-on-utah-in-redistricting-war-00535242" target="_blank">a safe Democratic seat</a>; and Republicans are facing <a href="https://apnews.com/article/missouri-redistricting-referendum-trump-gerrymandering-utah-14312a112b6e32d15e5ef36b83cdc6a7" target="_blank">a potential setback for Missouri</a>.</p> 
<p>That doesn’t mean the redistricting wars are over. Lawmakers in a number of other states are still weighing their own maps, with GOP-led Florida and Democratic-controlled Virginia remaining the biggest question marks on the board. Republicans are still eyeing Kentucky and Nebraska as well.</p> 
<p>“We’ve got a lot more states that we can do work on,” one person close to the White House, granted anonymity to speak candidly on a sensitive matter, told POLITICO on Friday, while admitting that “Indiana was definitely frustrating.”</p> 
<p>And if the U.S. Supreme Court issues a ruling <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/10/15/supreme-court-voting-rights-act-argument-00608340" target="_blank">further gutting the Voting Rights Act</a> in the coming months, a number of states are expected to rush to redraw their lines before their states’ filing deadlines, in a move that could give the GOP a huge boost and potentially put the House out of reach for Democrats.</p> 
<p>“The truth is, I think we're still, we're in the middle of this redistricting war,” said John Bisognano, president of the National Democratic Redistricting Committee. “We're all waiting to hear back from the Supreme Court as to what they're going to do and how they're going to move forward.”</p> 
<p>Here’s what to expect in the coming weeks from states including Maryland, Florida, Illinois and a challenge to the already-passed maps passed in Missouri.</p> 
<h5 class="story-text__heading-medium">Maryland</h5> 
<p>Perhaps lawmakers breathing the biggest sigh of relief from Indiana bucking Trump’s redistricting push are those in Maryland.</p> 
<p>Ferguson has for months been <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/02/bill-ferguson-wes-moore-00664770" target="_blank">facing pressure from Democratic Maryland Gov. Wes Moore and national Democrats</a> to wade into the redistricting fight. That lobbying campaign to net Maryland Democrats an additional seat would have been kicked into hyperdrive if Indiana had drawn new maps.</p> 
<p>Reports of Ferguson <a href="https://www.baltimoresun.com/2025/11/21/bill-ferguson-will-diminish-his-power-by-opposing-redistricting-analysts-say/" target="_blank">possibly losing his grip on leading the Senate Democrats</a> evaporated this week after he was <a href="https://www.baltimoresun.com/2025/12/10/bill-ferguson-renominated-legislative-session/?fbclid=IwY2xjawOpOLBleHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETFpNWo2dzNsRFZvdHByOHZrc3J0YwZhcHBfaWQQMjIyMDM5MTc4ODIwMDg5MgABHsvb6X9YnayF_uyDbyjoRCJBqoW_IJKgjHv21HFlMyqvJv9EhuGdfgoGjihO_aem_8OB90-oRdUVSdT3KZ-WpJQ" target="_blank">unanimously renominated as Senate leader</a>. Then on Thursday, just hours before the Indiana Senate cast the vote dooming the redistricting effort, Ferguson put out a statement with Democratic House Delegates Speaker Pro Tem Dana Stein declaring that lawmakers in the special session Moore called for next week <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/11/maryland-redistricting-state-legislature-00687992" target="_blank">will definitively not take up any new maps</a>.</p> 
<p>While that likely closes the door on the redistricting push for this year, Moore still has an opportunity to reignite a pressure campaign aimed at Ferguson to hold a vote on the issue in January, when the legislature returns for regular session. The governor’s <a href="https://redistricting.maryland.gov/Pages/default.aspx" target="_blank">Redistricting Advisory Commission</a> is meeting Friday for its final public hearing to solicit comments from Maryland residents before its members make a recommendation to the governor and the General Assembly on whether to redraw maps.</p> 
<h5 class="story-text__heading-medium">Illinois</h5> 
<p>For months, Illinois Democrats have suggested they were unlikely to try to squeeze another seat out of their already-gerrymandered state unless Indiana Republicans redrew their seats.</p> 
<p>And while state Democratic leaders didn’t completely rule out redistricting in the wake of the Indiana GOP’s vote, they don’t sound particularly eager for a new map.</p> 
<p>“Our neighbors in Indiana have stood up to Trump’s threats and political pressure, instead choosing to do what’s right for their constituents and our democracy,” Gov. JB Pritzker said in a statement after the result, without saying what Illinois might do.</p> 
<p>A person in Pritzker’s office, granted anonymity because they weren’t authorized to speak publicly, said the governor was less than equivocal in his statement because no one knows what Trump's next move might be.</p> 
<p>State House and Senate Democratic leaders struck similar tones, praising their Hoosier neighbors while pledging to stay vigilant against similar efforts in other states.</p> 
<h5 class="story-text__heading-medium">Virginia</h5> 
<p>Democrats’ best remaining chance for a multi-seat gerrymander is Old Dominion. But while statehouse leaders seem eager to push forward with a complicated plan for a voter referendum to approve a new gerrymander — much like California’s move — the state’s incoming Democratic governor doesn’t seem quite as eager to lend a hand.</p> 
<p>The Democratic-dominated Virginia legislature is expected to easily pass a procedural measure before putting the issue of redistricting before voters to approve a constitutional Virginia amendment to redraw the state’s maps ahead of the midterms — a move that legislative leaders have teased could lead to a 10-1 map.</p> 
<p>“I feel comfortable that we have an opportunity to do a number of maps here in Virginia to allow for us to level the playing field,” Virginia House Speaker Don Scott <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/09/virginia-house-speaker-optimism-supreme-court-00684139" target="_blank">said at a POLITICO event this week</a>.</p> 
<p>But at the same event, Spanberger hedged when asked if she supported redrawing maps to achieve the feat.</p> 
<p>“The calendar is tight, and for me, I want to win,” Spanberger said, pointing to Virginia’s first and second congressional districts that are currently held by Republicans. “I want to flip seats in the House of Representatives, and I know that we can because I just won those districts.”</p> 
<p>But when asked directly if redistricting is the way to go, Spanberger said that Virginia should “leave open the option” of new maps but that ultimately voters will decide if the legislature should move forward.</p> 
<h5 class="story-text__heading-medium">Florida</h5> 
<p>Florida Republicans could deliver their party three to five more seats if they press ahead with mid-decade redistricting. But two factors complicate that effort.</p> 
<p>First, GOP leaders aren’t on the same page. GOP Gov. Ron DeSantis has <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/07/24/desantis-floats-mid-decade-redistricting-for-florida-congressional-seats-00474675" target="_blank">been touting the need to draw new maps since last summer</a>, has suggested waiting until the spring of next year in case the U.S. Supreme Court weakens the Voting Rights Act and bars the consideration of race when drawing lines, a position backed by the state’s GOP Senate president, Ben Albritton.</p> 
<p>But state GOP House Speaker Daniel Perez said this week it is “irresponsible” to wait and that the House is prepared to send a map to the Senate during its regular session that starts next month.</p> 
<p>Second, GOP leaders may be constrained by Florida’s voter-approved constitutional ban on redistricting for partisan gain. Democrats have already asserted that drawing up any new map is “illegal’ and would violate these standards signaling that litigation is likely if state legislators pass a new map. But Florida's conservative-dominated state Supreme Court already ruled in 2022 that legislators can sidestep minority protections when it allowed a previous GOP-drawn map that was muscled into law by DeSantis, weakening its impact. </p> 
<p>Perez <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/10/florida-gop-house-speaker-insists-white-house-is-not-pushing-redistricting-00684542" target="_blank">insisted that he has not been under pressure from Trump or the White House to move ahead</a> on redistricting. When asked Friday if there was added pressure on the House to act due to the outcome in Indiana he said: “No sir.”</p> 
<h5 class="story-text__heading-medium">Missouri</h5> 
<p>Missouri Republicans already passed a map to flip Rep. <a href="https://directory.politicopro.com/member/51324">Emanuel Cleaver</a>’s (D-Mo.) district red, but Democrats are hoping to undo the GOP-passed map in Missouri via ballot measure. Earlier this week, they <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/09/missouri-gerrymandering-midterm-elections-00683693?nid=0000014f-1646-d88f-a1cf-5f46b7bd0000&amp;nname=playbook&amp;nrid=e43f4a81-fe4f-4770-8d77-d9774bf5ba97" target="_blank">submitted more than double</a> the 107,000 signatures required to force a statewide vote for the secretary of state.</p> 
<p>If the signatures are validated, the map may not cannot go into effect in time for the midterms, and if voters approve the ballot measure, the map gets tossed. Republicans still have a bit of time, since GOP Secretary of State Denny Hoskins doesn’t have to approve the signatures until July. Plus, it’s unclear when the Republican-controlled Legislature will actually put those signatures up for a vote.</p> 
<p>The timing is causing a bit of chaos. Since candidates need to file by the end of March, prospective members of Congress may have to file in districts that aren’t set for the midterms.</p>
