## Content

<p>When I visited the Snapple website this week, I was served one of the drink brand’s famous fun facts: that a jiffy is an “actual time measurement equaling 1/100th of a second.” Fun indeed! And arguably even a little bit true!</p><p>In 2013 in <em>The Atlantic</em>, <a href="https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2013/10/we-fact-checked-snapples-real-facts/280512/?utm_source=feed">Adrienne LaFrance courageously exposed</a> that many of Snapple’s bottle-cap facts were false. I wished to determine whether the company had since cleaned up its act; the intervening rebrand from “Real Facts” (scare quotes theirs) to Real Facts (no quotes!) felt like a vow.</p><p>Alas, a <em>jiffy</em> was first proposed as the name for the time light takes to travel one centimeter in a vacuum—about 33 <em>trillionths </em>of a second. In electronics, a jiffy might be 1/50 or 1/60 of a second. Only in Linux computing specifically can it be 1/100 of a second, and even then only sometimes.</p><p>Oh well. I did not have the heart to check the next statement: “Mangoes can get sunburned.” Whatever, sure.
